Teague Electric
Potential M&A Target Map

Hugo Nascimento · November 2026

IX and Align backed Teague in May 2025 with a clear plan: grow it organically and buy electrical services companies in Kansas City and the neighboring regions. I kept wondering what the right first add-on would actually look like.

My starting point was that most electrical shops would make Teague bigger in the same Johnson County zip codes, doing the same work. That isn't necessarily a bad thing, but the more interesting deals add something Teague doesn't have yet. So I worked backwards. I mapped what Teague does today, found the places it looks thin (at least from the outside looking in), and only then went looking for companies that fill those holes.

I screened about 80 businesses using Google Maps, generator dealer listings, chamber and trade directories, state filings and job postings. I set aside the ones that have already sold, are franchises, or are too big to be an add-on, and ranked the remaining 29 on five things: which gaps they fill, signs the owner is nearing retirement, size, independence and reputation.

Figures here are estimates based on public data and could be incorrect.

Teague Today

Harold Teague founded the company in 1978. It runs from one building in Lenexa with roughly 165 to 195 people, which makes it one of the largest independent electrical contractors in Kansas City. The center of gravity is commercial construction, a 50-plus person department, along with Teague Critical Systems for data center, healthcare and correctional work.

Around that core sit residential new construction, residential service, underground work that Evergy refers, a 14-person low-voltage and smart-home division (Teague iCtrl), and a generator division being built out in 2026. Teague is a Kohler Platinum and Generac Select dealer.

The reputation is a real asset: 4.9 stars on 718 Google reviews, 4.8 on GuildQuality, BBB A+ and Great Place to Work certified. Whoever joins Teague inherits that standard, so culture fit matters as much as capability.

How This Was Built

Sources

  • Teague's site, team pages, job postings and dealer listings
  • Google Maps searches across the eight markets, with ratings and review counts as of November 2026
  • Company websites, BBB records, local press, chamber and trade directories

Fit Score (100 Points)

High retirement signal means a founder or long-time owner past about 30 years with no visible successor. Unknown scores low on purpose so verified names rise.

Caveats

  • Revenue is estimated at about $150K to $200K per employee for electrical contractors unless a directory figure is cited.
  • Founding year stands in for owner age.
  • Several owners are unconfirmed. Kansas and Missouri Secretary of State records are the next step.
  • Nothing here says any of these owners want to sell.
